Bathroom waterproofing in Newcastle

In short: Waterproofing is one of the most important parts of a bathroom renovation. In NSW it is specialist work that must be done by, or subcontracted to, a licensed waterproofer.

What to know and what to ask, with links to official NSW sources.

Last updated: 23 November 2026

This guide explains why waterproofing matters and what NSW rules say. It is not technical or legal advice; the licensed waterproofer and renovator are responsible for compliant work.

Why waterproofing matters

Water escaping through a shower or bathroom floor can rot framing, damage walls and ceilings, and lead to mould, often long before you see it. Failed waterproofing is expensive to fix because tiles and fixtures have to come out. That's why it is one of the most important steps in a bathroom renovation, even though you never see it once the tiles are on.

The standard

The National Construction Code requires wet areas in homes to be waterproofed, and the Australian Standard for waterproofing domestic wet areas is AS 3740. Ask the renovator which parts of the room will be waterproofed and to what height, and what products and system they use.

Licensed waterproofing in NSW

In NSW, waterproofing is specialist work. The NSW Government says a contractor needs a licence for waterproofing work valued at more than $5,000, and a kitchen, bathroom and laundry renovator must subcontract specialist work such as waterproofing to a contractor licensed for it.

Sources: NSW Government, Waterproofing work; NSW Government, Kitchen, bathroom and laundry renovation work.

Questions to ask

  • Who will do the waterproofing, and what licence do they hold?
  • Will I get a waterproofing certificate when it's done?
  • How long does the membrane cure before tiling?
  • Is a flood test or inspection part of the process?
  • What warranty covers the waterproofing?

Older bathrooms in Newcastle

The housing mix runs from Victorian terraces and workers' cottages in Cooks Hill, Hamilton and Mayfield, through post-war brick and fibro homes in the western suburbs, to newer estates around Fletcher and Minmi. Older bathrooms were often built before current waterproofing standards, so a renovation is a good time to bring the wet areas up to date.
